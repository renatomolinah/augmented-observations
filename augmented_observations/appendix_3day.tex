\section{Stage 1 with 3-Day-Ahead Forecasts Only} \label{app:3day}

Our headline Stage 1 estimates use the forecast error averaged over the 1 to 3 day lead window, following \citet{molina2024social}. Because the social value of augmented observations concentrates at the 3-day lead, we verify that the estimated marginal cost of forecast error does not depend on this averaging. We re-estimate the preferred column (7) specification using the 3-day-ahead forecast error alone, rebuilt from the raw per-lead forecasts and demeaned within the same realized-wind bins, while holding the cost outcomes, fixed effects, and intensity controls fixed.

Table \ref{tab:appB_3day} reports the result. The marginal cost of a hurricane-force forecast error remains positive, large, and statistically significant at \$3.80 million per unit (SE = 1.85), against \$5.03 million in the pooled-lead baseline; the two estimates are statistically indistinguishable. As in the main specification, the effect for sub-hurricane-force winds is small and statistically indistinguishable from zero. The 3-day sample is smaller (75,512 versus 113,661 county observations) because the 3-day forecast footprint is narrower than the pooled-lead window. The central finding, that the cost of forecast error is concentrated in high-intensity events and economically substantial, is robust to using only 3-day-ahead forecasts.
